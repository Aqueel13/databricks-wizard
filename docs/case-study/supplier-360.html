One supplier, three systems, two scripts

Databricks Wizard is a governed Supplier 360 for Larkspur Foods, a fictional food manufacturer. It resolves SAP, Coupa and HICX supplier records and supplier documents into one golden record. People and AI agents then work from the same trusted data.

The problem

Every supplier number is wrong until the records agree

Spend per supplier is split across duplicates. Delivery performance can't be compared. A certificate's expiry date in the supplier portal drifts from the PDF. Polish and Ukrainian buyers type names in their own scripts, while group systems store English transliterations. AI assistants either can't see this data or see it ungoverned.

The project builds the data foundation that fixes this, at the data layer, on a free platform. Every quality claim is measured against an answer key of planted problems: duplicates, transliterations, ID typos, conflicting addresses, delivery drift, expired certificates and sanctions near-matches.

What it taught

Four things that changed the design

The LLM made matching worse until it was guarded

Unguarded, it matched one record to several suppliers and chained clusters together. Treating "matches many" as ambiguity, and sending it to a steward, fixed it.

The answer key was wrong before the matcher was

SAP training copies of one vendor were grouped inconsistently. Correcting the key without touching the data was worth more than any tuning.

A missing tool, not a weak model

Every model failed the spend-concentration question by writing broken SQL. One governed tool fixed it for all of them.

Market-adjusted, or it's noise

The market's on-time rate swings between 31% and 77% month to month. Supplier deterioration is measured against peers, and it also found real declines in untouched SAP history.

Agents and people

Agents propose, people decide

Three MCP servers (analytics, knowledge, stewardship) expose governed tools. Every tool returns provenance: the SQL run, the tables read, the document page cited. Every call is audited under the caller's identity. An agent can propose a steward decision, but only a person can approve it. A sanctions alert is a name similarity for review, and no tool presents it as a finding.
